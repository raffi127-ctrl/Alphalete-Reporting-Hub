Office Message Setup

A proposed addition to AppStream AI Settings. One form per office, filled in once by the ICD.

Why this solves the validator problem. Today the validator rejects specific claims because it cannot tell whether they are true for a given office. If the office supplies the facts and signs off on the wording, they are verified by definition — the system generates the messages from what was entered, the ICD approves each one, and nothing has to be argued with sentence by sentence. Fields marked NEW do not exist today.

Office details

We do not recommend using one of your recruiting team member's names. An escalation only works if it hands the applicant to someone new. If the AI texts as Dani and then tells the applicant that Dani will call them, you have just handed them to the person they were already talking to. Make one up — it is the one field on this page where you get to have fun.

Who the AI is

Two names, doing two different jobs. The first is a persona the AI texts under. The second is a real person on your team — the one who will actually pick up the phone.

Pick one of the suggestions or type your own. Not "Hiring Manager" — that is the ICD, who is never on the phone with an applicant before an interview, so the title promises a call from someone who will not make it. Use the title that fits whoever actually rings them.

Timing

These two settings decide something easy to miss: how long an applicant actually has to take the time they were offered. Both are counted backwards from the interview, so they only make sense together.

Your prep time. At 60, someone texting at 9:30 is offered 10:30 as the earliest.

How close to the interview someone can still accept. Counted backwards, so a bigger number gives them less time. At 5 they can take a 10:30 slot up until 10:25.

Silence this long puts a ghost icon on the thread so someone can re-engage. Most applicants take around an hour to reply, so we do not suggest anything shorter than 60 — below that you are flagging people who are simply busy.

Where to start: 60 / 5 / 60. That offers an interview an hour out and gives the applicant 55 minutes to take it. An hour keeps it same day, which matters more than it sounds: measured across 4,500 interviews at one office, same-day bookings showed up 57% of the time against 42% the next day and 38% two to three days out. Pushing the first slot further out to buy a bigger window costs more than it gains.

If your team cannot take someone accepting five minutes before the slot starts, raise the cut-off to 15 — you still get a 45-minute window.
