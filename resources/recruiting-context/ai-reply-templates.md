## 1. Can we do this time?

**Asked 116 times** in the week to 2026-09-25, across 11280 / 11580 / 23965 / 24065.

> Answer the time they NAMED before offering anything else.

- *“Can we do 11am instead?”*
- *“Hello! Thank you for reaching out. I'd be available around 10 am. Would that work for you?”*
- *“Can you add me for 1:45 please”*

**Replies**

1. Open: “{namedTime} works — you’re locked in, see you then!”
   Taken: “Ah, {namedTime} is already taken — but I have {slotOffer}. Would either work?”

2. Open:  “Perfect, {namedTime} is open — you're all set. See you then!”
   Taken: “{namedTime} was just taken — I've got {slotOffer} open. Which would you prefer?”

3. Open:  “Yes, {namedTime} works. I've got you down — talk soon!”
   Taken: “{namedTime} isn't free, unfortunately — but I have {slotOffer}. Do either of those work for you?”

4. Open:  “{namedTime} it is — you're booked in. Looking forward to it!”
   Taken: “Someone just took {namedTime}. I can do {slotOffer} — does one of those work?”

5. Open:  “Great, {namedTime} is available — I've locked it in for you!”
   Taken: “{namedTime} is spoken for, but I have {slotOffer}. Can you commit to either?”

**Routing:** Don't Escalate — After we confirm their time they usually reply again — the AI needs to still be there.

**Rules**

- Answer the time they NAMED before offering anything else — leading with alternatives reads as not listening. But never offer the slot CLOSEST to their time: if they ask for a day out, the nearest slot is still a day out. Any slot we propose sits 3-6 hours from now, because booked 3-6 hours ahead showed at 59%, against 45% inside 3 hours and 36% a day or more out. A request for a day further out is its own card — see “Can we do a day further out?”.

## 2. They want a day past tomorrow

**Asked 37 times** in the week to 2026-09-25, across 11280 / 11580 / 23965 / 24065.

> Today or tomorrow, the AI books it. Past tomorrow: give a reason, offer two real times, and escalate only if neither works.

- *“Am sorry got some unexpected things, can we do it on Monday?”*
- *“Hi! could i possibly reschedule for wednesday morning?”*
- *“Can we do thursday”*

**Replies**

1. “Ah, {namedTime} is a tricky one — we're wrapping up interviews for this role. I can do {slotOffer} though. If neither works I'll put you in touch with the {escalationTitle} to see what we can accommodate. Which would you prefer?”

2. “I'm sorry, {namedTime} isn't on the schedule — we're finishing up interviews for this role. I do have {slotOffer}. If those don't suit, I can get the {escalationTitle} to look at other options. Would either of those work for you?”

3. “Unfortunately {namedTime} isn't available — we're closing out interviews for this role. The times I have are {slotOffer}. If neither fits, the {escalationTitle} can see what else we can do. Which suits you better?”

4. “I don't have {namedTime} open — we're wrapping up interviews for this role. I can offer {slotOffer} though! If neither works for you, I'll ask the {escalationTitle} what else we can do. Do either of those work?”

5. “Sorry, I can't book {namedTime} — we're finishing interviews for this role. I have {slotOffer} available. If those don't work I'll get the {escalationTitle} to reach out about other times. Can you do either of those?”

**Routing:** Don't Escalate — We're offering times and waiting on an answer, so the AI has to stay in the conversation. It only hands over if they turn both down.

**Rules**

- Never hand this straight to a person — a bare handoff reads as a deflection, which is the thing ruling 4 bans. The shape is: a REASON the day isn't available, two real times, the {escalationTitle} as a fallback if neither works, and a question (Megan, 2026-10-26). “We're wrapping up interviews for this role” is a STANDING line, not a per-office fact — hiring runs in continuous cycles, so it always holds, and it is deliberate fear of loss (Megan, 2026-10-26). Offered times sit 3-6 hours out: booked 3-6 hours ahead showed at 59%, a day or two out at 36%, two or more days out at 32%. If they turn down both times, it escalates to a person straight away — one refusal here, not two. Never put a clock on the handoff, and never frame the interview as a chore.

**Shortcodes still to build:** `{escalationTitle}`, `{namedTime}`, `{slotOffer}`

**Build notes:** This row is TWO steps, and AppStream has to know about both:

  - STEP 1 — a new SCHEDULING row that fires when the day the applicant asks for is NEITHER today NOR tomorrow. The AI compares the requested date against today's, gives the reason, and offers two times.
  - Route STEP 1 as Don't Escalate, so the AI stays in the conversation to take their answer.
  - STEP 2 — if the applicant turns down BOTH offered times, escalate to a person. This must fire after ONE rejection here, not two. The existing Scheduling Impasse row needs two or more sets of times refused, which is too late for this case: they have already asked for something we cannot do, so the first refusal is the end of what the AI can usefully try.
  - The handoff message on step 2 names the {escalationTitle} and carries no time word — never “shortly” or “soon”.
  - Today and tomorrow keep the current behaviour — card 1 handles those.

## 3. What times do you have?

**Asked 66 times** in the week to 2026-09-25, across 11280 / 11580 / 23965 / 24065.

> They are asking us to propose a time. Give real times — never hand the question back.

- *“Yes I am what days are available ?”*
- *“That's great! When is the next available time that works for you?”*
- *“Do you have any times available tomorrow”*

**Replies**

1. “Good timing — it looks like we're wrapping up interviews for this role, but we can do {slotOffer}{soonerLine}. Which would be best for your schedule?”

2. “We're finishing up interviews for this role, but I can offer {slotOffer}{soonerLine}. Which fits your schedule better?”

3. “We're closing out interviews for this role, though I can do {slotOffer}{soonerLine}. Which works best for you?”

4. “Interviews for this role are wrapping up, but I've still got {slotOffer}{soonerLine}. Which works for your schedule?”

5. “We're near the end of interviews for this role, but I still have {slotOffer}{soonerLine}. Which would work best for you?”

**Routing:** Don't Escalate — They pick one of the times next, so the AI has to be there to take it.

**Rules**

- Open with the urgency, give TWO real times, then ask which fits (Megan, 2026-10-26). Never answer this with “what works for you?” — they just asked us that.
- WHICH TIMES TO OFFER: • No day named — the next two slots 3-6 hours out. • They named TODAY or TOMORROW — two slots ON THAT DAY. Answering “any times tomorrow?” with today's times ignores the word they used. • A day PAST TOMORROW — card 2.
- ALWAYS SURFACE THE SOONER SLOT (Megan, 2026-10-26). Whenever something earlier than the day they asked about is open, {soonerLine} adds it. Give them their day AND the better one, then let them choose — same day shows at 57% against 42% next day, and 3-6 hours out at 59%. We never argue them out of their choice, we just make the better option visible.
- “We're wrapping up interviews for this role” is the approved standing line. If they ask a second question alongside, answer both.

## 4. Can I reschedule?

**Asked 81 times** in the week to 2026-09-25, across 11280 / 11580 / 23965 / 24065.

> A bare request to move it — no time named, no day named.

- *“Can I reschedule?”*
- *“Could we try to reschedule the interview?”*
- *“Is there any way that I could reschedule”*

**Replies**

1. Offer:   “Of course I can move it, {applicantFirstName}. We're wrapping up interviews for this role, so I have {slotOffer}. Which one can you commit to?”
   Confirm: “Perfect — you're locked in for {chosenSlot}. See you then!”
   If no:   “No problem — I'll have the {escalationTitle} reach out to see if we can find something that works.”

2. Offer:   “Sure — though interviews for this role are wrapping up, so I have {slotOffer}. Can you commit to one of those?”
   Confirm: “Locked in for {chosenSlot}. See you then!”
   If no:   “Totally understand. I'll pass you to the {escalationTitle} to see if we can work out a time for you.”

3. Offer:   “I can do that. We're near the end of interviews for this role, so it's down to {slotOffer}. Which can you lock in?”
   Confirm: “You're all set for {chosenSlot}. Talk soon!”
   If no:   “No worries at all — the {escalationTitle} will get in touch to see if we can find you a slot.”

4. Offer:   “Of course. Interviews for this role are wrapping up though — I have {slotOffer}. Which can you make?”
   Confirm: “Great — {chosenSlot} it is. Looking forward to it!”
   If no:   “No problem. I'll let the {escalationTitle} know so they can see what else might work.”

5. Offer:   “I can move it. We're finishing interviews for this role, so I have {slotOffer}. Which can you commit to?”
   Confirm: “You're booked in for {chosenSlot}. Looking forward to it!”
   If no:   “Got it — I'll have the {escalationTitle} contact you to see if we can find a better time.”

**Routing:** Don't Escalate — They pick a time next, so the AI has to still be there to take it.

**Rules**

- Posture, don't just hand it over (Megan, 2026-10-26). Moving an interview is something we can do, not something we are grateful to be asked for — “Of course! Happy to!” reads as needing them. The shape: a short acknowledgement, the scarcity line, the two times, and a question that asks them to COMMIT rather than merely prefer. That last word does the work — “which can you commit to?” gets a decision where “which do you like?” gets a maybe.
- If they turn down BOTH times, escalate — one refusal, not two. The AI has nothing left to try, and “what works for you?” is the bounce-back we banned. A no here usually means they need a different day, which is card 2's situation anyway.
- Warm up once they have committed. The confirmation is where the friendliness goes — posture before, warmth after.
- If they name a time, that is card 1. A day, card 5. Past tomorrow, card 2.

**Shortcodes still to build:** `{chosenSlot}`, `{escalationTitle}`, `{slotOffer}`

**Build notes:** One row, and it is not the same thing as their existing reschedule templates:

  - A new SCHEDULING row for a plain reschedule request — no time named, no day named. Their existing Same day / Next day text templates are manual recruiter inserts, not AI rows, so they do not cover this.
  - Route it Don't Escalate so the AI offers times and stays in the conversation to take the answer.
  - If the applicant turns down BOTH offered times, escalate to a person — after ONE refusal, not two. Scheduling Impasse needs two or more sets refused, which is too slow here.
  - Request Outside Available Timeframes stays as it is — that is the SECOND-round reply after a first offer is refused, not the opening one. Its current message also reads “an spot”.

## 5. Can we do today or tomorrow?

**Asked 35 times** in the week to 2026-09-25, across 11280 / 11580 / 23965 / 24065.

> They named a day but no time — and the day is today or tomorrow, so the AI books it.

- *“Anything today ?”*
- *“Can we do it tomorrow?”*

**Replies**

1. “I can definitely do {namedDay}, {applicantFirstName}. We're wrapping up interviews for this role, so I have {slotOffer}. Which one can you commit to?”
   That day won't work: “I don't have anything {namedDay}, but I've got {nextInterviewDay} — {slotTimes}. Would either work?”

2. “{namedDay} works — interviews for this role are wrapping up though, so it's down to {slotOffer}. Which can you lock in?”
   That day won't work: “Nothing open {namedDay}, though I have {nextInterviewDay} at {slotTimes}. Which can you lock in?”

3. “I can get you in {namedDay}. We're near the end of interviews for this role, so I have {slotOffer}. Which can you make?”
   That day won't work: “I can't do {namedDay}, but I can get you in {nextInterviewDay} — {slotTimes}. Which can you make?”

4. “Sure, I have some openings {namedDay}. We're finishing up interviews for this role, so I've got {slotTimes}. Which can you commit to?”
   That day won't work: “{namedDay} isn't one I can do, but I have openings {nextInterviewDay} at {slotTimes}. Which can you commit to?”

5. “Yes — I can do {namedDay}! Interviews are wrapping up for this role, so I have {slotOffer}. Which works best for you?”
   That day won't work: “I don't have {namedDay}, though I've got {nextInterviewDay} — {slotTimes}. Which works best for you?”

**Routing:** Don't Escalate — They pick a time next, so the AI has to still be there to take it.

**Rules**

- They named the day, so name it back — then give that day's times. Offering whatever is next without acknowledging the day ignores what they said, the same fault as card 1. Keep the scarcity line and ask them to COMMIT, not to prefer. Never say the day is “open” — that claims the whole day is free and contradicts the scarcity line in the same breath. Where a variation names the day in prose, use {slotTimes} so the day is not repeated.
- NOT EVERY OFFICE INTERVIEWS EVERY DAY (Megan, 2026-10-26). If the day they asked for is not one this office runs interviews on, NEVER say so — “we don't run interviews Thursday” exposes the machinery and makes us sound like a conveyor belt (Megan, 2026-10-26). Frame it as capacity, which is also true: “I don't have anything {namedDay}”. Then offer {nextInterviewDay}. AppStream already carries {currentInterviewDay} and {nextInterviewDay}, so the office's own schedule drives this — nothing new to build.
- This is also why the rule is the next available INTERVIEW days, not literally today and tomorrow. An office running Tuesdays and Thursdays would escalate every single applicant under a calendar-day rule.

**Shortcodes still to build:** `{namedDay}`, `{slotOffer}`, `{slotTimes}`

**Build notes:** One row, plus the same escalation step as card 4:

  - A new SCHEDULING row that fires when the applicant names a DAY with no time, and that day is today or tomorrow.
  - The AI has to read the named day and offer slots ON it — not whatever is next on the calendar.
  - Route Don't Escalate so the AI stays in to take their choice.
  - If they turn down both times, escalate after ONE refusal, same as card 4.

## Copy standard — check every reply against this

> Every variation on every card gets read against these before it ships. Most are quick; the two marked ⚠ are the ones that actually catch things.

### The basics

- **Grammatically correct** — Read it aloud. “Do either work?” is wrong — “either” is singular. Card 1 now uses “Do either of those work for you?”, which reads most naturally in a text.
- **Friendly, a little excited, humour welcome** — Energy about the OPPORTUNITY, never gratitude to the applicant — excited and needy are different things (Megan, 2026-10-26). Put the enthusiasm where it fits: “we'd love to get you in” dropped into an address answer reads as filler and undoes the posture. HUMOUR IS ABOUT US OR THE SITUATION, NEVER ABOUT THEM. A text carries no tone, so anything that comments on the applicant lands as a dig — “nice detective work” for guessing a city reads as sarcasm, and so does “not far off your guess”. Keep it warm and aimed at the circumstance. And never in all five: one flat joke out of five is recoverable, five is not.
- **Warmth is the default, not a pass** — Friendly and a little excited is how every reply gets written the first time — it is not a later sweep over cold drafts (Megan, 2026-10-27). Nothing is flagged as “warmed up” because nothing ships cold.
- **Friendly, and professional with it** — Warm without being chummy. No slang, no exclamation marks stacked up, no corporate stiffness either.
- **Clear and concise** — One idea per sentence. If a line needs re-reading, rewrite it.
- **It actually answers what they asked** — The commonest failure in the whole audit: 376 of 817 replies were typed, polite, and answered nothing.
- **Ends somewhere useful** — A question that moves it forward, or a clear confirmation. Never a dead end.
- **Answer BOTH questions if they asked two** — Applicants bundle: “What time slots do you have? Also can you remind me of the role?” Answering one and ignoring the other is how a reply reads as an automated blast rather than a person.
- **Posture — never read as grateful** — “Of course! Happy to!” signals we need them. Acknowledge briefly, keep the scarcity, and ask them to COMMIT rather than to prefer. Indifference is the I in FUGI. Save the warmth for after they have said yes. And never make the next step sound like a chore. “Knock this out”, “get it out of the way” — that is how you describe something nobody wants to do (Megan). This is a job they applied for.
- **Watch the curt acknowledgement** — “That's fine” and “Understood” read as annoyance in a text — the same words that are neutral out loud go flat on a screen, with no tone to carry them. “No problem”, “No worries at all”, “Totally understand” and “Of course” all do the same job warmly. Posture is about not sounding grateful, never about sounding short.
- **Keep the register American and plain** — “Suits”, “sort it” and “I'm afraid” read as British and land oddly in a Texas recruiting text. Plainer equivalents: works for you, work it out, unfortunately. Also “the detail” as a mass noun — “we get into the detail” is British. American English wants “the details” (Megan, 2026-10-29).
- **Don't contradict the scarcity you just claimed** — “Tomorrow is open” says the whole day is free, one clause before “we're wrapping up interviews”. Say what we HAVE — “I have some openings tomorrow” — not that the day is empty.
- **Ask for commitment with a conditional** — “Can you make that commute IF OFFERED A POSITION?” beats “can you commit to that commute?” twice over: the conditional removes the pressure to decide now, and “if offered” implies the role is not theirs yet. Commitment without cornering them.
- **Don't reopen what they already decided** — Once someone is booked, asking them to commit again reopens the decision — “Can you commit to that time?” invites a no from a person who had already said yes (Megan, 2026-10-27). Past the booking, close on a small ask that assumes they are coming: be on early, test the link, find a quiet spot. Ask for commitment only when nothing is booked yet.
- **Never sound like we recruit in bulk** — Anything that implies volume gives it away: “Happens all the time” says we text so many people that this is routine, and “we don't run interviews on Thursdays” says we batch them (Megan, 2026-10-27). Keep every reply sounding like it is about this one person and this one role. Scarcity is the whole posture, and bulk is the opposite of scarce. The fix is to put the warmth on THEIR side of it instead of ours: “Ha, no worries — job hunting can be overwhelming!” says the same kind thing without telling them how many people we text (Megan, 2026-10-27).
- **Remember where they are standing** — Every one of these messages arrives from a phone, usually from someone at work. “Will your phone be handy?” to a person texting us from that phone reads as not paying attention (Megan, 2026-10-27). The real obstacles are a shift they cannot leave, somewhere too loud to talk, and a number their handset screens out.

### ⚠ The two that catch real problems

- **NEVER claim a distance — we don't know where they are** — “The closest I have”, “Close —”, “not too far”, “not far from there at all”: every one asserts something about THEIR geography that we cannot know. Someone who guessed Houston gets told it is not far. This has crept back in three times — on slots, on cities, and on the warmth pass — so treat any word about nearness as a defect on sight. State where WE are and let them judge the drive.
- **Only claims what we KNOW — except deliberate urgency** — Do not invent facts. Two approved exceptions, both deliberate fear of loss (Megan, 2026-10-26): scarcity on a taken slot — “was just taken”, “someone just took it” — and “we're wrapping up interviews for this role”, which is a standing line because hiring runs in continuous cycles. Everything else stands: never invent anything about pay, the role, the address, the schedule or their application.
- **Reads right once the shortcodes fill in** — Test it with a real value. {namedTime} might be “11am” or “Wednesday at 6pm” — the sentence has to work with both.
- **A shortcode holding “X or Y” needs a construction that fits a PAIR** — Two ways it breaks, both only visible once filled in. As the subject: “{slotOffer} are still open” — wrong verb, and “is” is no better. After “it's”: “so it's {slotOffer}” reads as “so it's today at 2:15 or 3:45”, which points at one thing. Use a verb that takes a pair — “I have {slotOffer}”, “it's down to {slotOffer}”.

### The house rules

- **Never state age, gender or who we hire** — Standing compliance rule. “Suit and tie” broke it.
- **No negations that plant the fear** — “No cold calling” makes them think about cold calling. Say what it IS.
- **Sentence case, never all caps** — The company name went out shouted on 40 of 40 sends in late September.
- **Never defer a job fact to a person** — Pay, hours, role, location and remote all have approved answers. Logistics may escalate; facts may not.
- **Never put a clock on a call** — “Shortly”, “soon”, “today” — nothing holds those promises.
- **Never promise an OUTCOME we don't control** — “The Hiring Manager will contact you to FIND a better time” promises one exists. “To SEE IF WE CAN find a better time” is the same handoff without the guarantee. Sister rule to never putting a clock on a call: we can promise the action, never the result (Megan, 2026-10-26).
- **Never expose the machinery** — “We don't run interviews on Thursdays”, “you're in the batch for”, “our system has you down as” — all tell the applicant they are being processed rather than considered. Speak as one person with a calendar: “I don't have anything Thursday” says the same thing and keeps it personal. Scarcity about OUR capacity is good; visible process is not.
- **Sequence the information — don't open doors early** — Every added detail invites its own question. “Where the team meets before heading out” answers a commute question and raises a bigger one: head out where? Even “that's where your day starts” begs “and then?”. Fold the fact into the question instead — “that DAILY commute” — and say nothing more. The harder facts have their own cards.

## Routing — which mode each row needs

> The four modes, in eStream's own words (Jovanna De La Torre). The mode decides whether the conversation survives the answer — and whether our approved wording survives it. Clarify and Escalate both send "a VARIATION of the custom message", so they paraphrase what we wrote. Only Don't Escalate sends it as written. On a project whose whole point is approved wording, that makes Don't Escalate the default and every other mode a deliberate exception.

### What the four modes do

- **Don't Escalate** — Sends the custom message AS WRITTEN and keeps the conversation going. The default for everything we have worded carefully.
- **Clarify** — Sends a VARIATION to seek clarification, and keeps going. Our exact wording is not what reaches them.
- **Escalate** — Sends a VARIATION, raises a flag, then STOPS all further communication. Right only where a person genuinely takes over.
- **Silent** — Stops all communication and raises a flag. Sends nothing — a message saved on a Silent row never fires.

### Needs changing on 11280

- **Compensation — now Escalate** — Should be Don't Escalate. Answering pay currently ends the conversation; the AI spoke again only 15% of the time.
- **Applicant Requested Directions — now Escalate** — Should be Don't Escalate. 0% of these saw the AI again — someone asking how to find us is about to attend.
- **Confusion About Role Type — now Escalate** — Should be Don't Escalate. It is an informational answer, not a handoff.
- **Mentions "Scam" — now Silent** — Sends nothing. Zero of nine answered, and all three who got silence never booked.
- **Non-English Language Detected — now Silent** — Sends nothing. Spanish is the 3rd most common thing applicants say.

### Correct as they are

- **Request to talk via Phone — Escalate** — A person really does take over, so the AI stopping is right. The copy must not promise an AI reply afterwards.
- **Remote Work — Clarify** — Answers and keeps going. 47% saw the AI again, the best of any row measured.
- **Job Description — Clarify** — Answers and keeps going.

### ✗ Questions we deliberately do NOT template

- **“Any updates?” / “Am I still being considered?”** — Escalate, no template. The answer is a judgement about one person — where they are, how the interview went, whether anyone is still deciding — and a recruiter owns that case by case (Megan, 2026-10-30). Asked once in the audit week. A generic reply here is either a false promise or a rejection by accident.
