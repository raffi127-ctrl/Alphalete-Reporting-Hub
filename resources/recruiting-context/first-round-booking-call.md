# First-round booking call — the quick pitch

The phone call that turns an applicant into a booked 1st-round interview.
This is the **front** of the pipeline; [hiring-call-scripts.md](hiring-call-scripts.md)
is the back of it (BOB, new start, parting ways).

Source: Megan asked in `#rafs-office-recruiting-11280` on 2026-10-08 for "the
script / any coaching material that you train on how to do phone calls to book
1st rounds on". **Dani Pena** replied 2026-10-09 with the script below and the
notes under it. Dani says *"The pitch Rafael provided is the one we've been
instructed to use for booking first-round interviews"* — so this is the
sanctioned pitch, not one person's version of it.

As with the other scripts, `X` is a placeholder. The times, email and the
recruiter's own name are filled in per call. [[feedback_house_rules_per_office]]

## The script

> Hey is this X?
>
> Awesome, this is X, I was calling about your job application you submitted to
> us, do you have a quick minute to chat?
>
> Awesome my manager wanted me to personally reach out to you and set you up for
> a quick zoom call to go over the role. Did you have some time for today we can
> meet?
>
> Awesome, can you make X time or X time work?
>
> Great, I'll go ahead and lock you in for this time, is X your correct email?
>
> Great, I'll send you a confirmation text and email with the time.
> Today/Tomorrow morning you'll also get the zoom link. So we'll see you then!
>
> Bye!

## How they are trained to run it

In Dani's words:

- **Read the resume first.** Identify the relevant skills that align with the
  role, and use them to make the conversation more engaging and personalised.
- **Enthusiasm, and smile while speaking.**
- **Use SEE factors** (written "C-factors" in the message) to build rapport and increase candidate commitment.
- **Leverage same-day opportunities, urgency, and FUGI** to get candidates to
  secure their spot and improve show-up rates.

### FUGI and the SEE factors

Both are trained direct-sales vocabulary rather than anything this pack
explains. Looked up 2026-10-09; Megan corrected the spelling of the second
one — Dani's message writes it "C-factors" but it is **SEE factors**.

**SEE — Smile, Eye contact, Enthusiasm.** From Smart Circle's own sales
training, which is the same direct-sales world Alphalete works in:

| | What it does |
|---|---|
| **S**mile | Signals you are open to contact; they smile back and open up too |
| **E**ye contact | Holding someone's gaze shows you have nothing to hide — trust and respect |
| **E**nthusiasm | Keeping the smile up and engaging fully, so they stay curious enough to have a real conversation |

Note what this means for a PHONE call: two of the three are visual. On a
Zoom or in person the recruiter gets all three; on the booking call the
applicant can only hear the smile and the enthusiasm. Dani's own note pairs
them exactly that way — *"maintaining enthusiasm, smiling while speaking"*.
So the gradeable part of SEE on a recorded call is **warmth and energy in
the voice**, not eye contact.

**FUGI — the four impulse factors.** Standard direct-sales shorthand, though
no single authoritative source owns it and it also circulates as **GUFI**:

| | What it leans on |
|---|---|
| **F**ear | Fear of *loss*, not fear generally — the spot, the slot, the chance goes to someone else |
| **U**rgency | A clock. Act before it runs out or miss it |
| **G**reed | Wanting the upside others are getting; social proof, "everyone is taking these" |
| **I**ndifference | Not appearing to need the sale. A neutral, unpressured posture that leaves the other person feeling in control |

Dani's note — *"leverage same-day opportunities, urgency, and FUGI"* — maps
onto U and onto F as fear of loss: book today, before the slot goes.

## Why this script matters to the audit

The SMS audit's sharpest finding, across all four accounts, is that **the
channel a booking comes from predicts whether the applicant turns up**:

| Booked by | Showed up |
|---|---|
| Text | 47% |
| Phone call | 14% |

The gap holds at every booking lead time, and it holds per recruiter — Jorge
Pena's six weeks run 59/52/47% by text against 27/15/14% by call. So the call
above is where the pipeline leaks hardest, and it is the thing the recruiter
scorecards now coach on by name.

Reading the script against that number, one thing stands out: **it is almost
entirely transactional.** Eight lines, of which six are logistics — confirm
identity, ask for a minute, name the Zoom, offer two times, confirm the email,
confirm the send. The only relationship-building is in the coaching notes around
it (read the resume, personalise, C-factors), not in the script itself.

That is an observation from the data, not a verdict on the script, and it is
exactly the hypothesis the Ringover call audit is being built to test: the
recordings and Empower transcripts will show whether the personalising actually
happens on the call, or whether the script gets read straight through.
